# Timezone Matching Strategy — PKT (UTC+5) for Global Remote Roles

Your timezone is not a weakness to apologize for. Positioned correctly, it is a **selling point**: you can cover EU afternoons AND US East mornings in the same working day — something a candidate sitting in London cannot offer.

---

## 1. Know your exact overlap numbers

Assuming a standard Lahore day of **09:00–18:00 PKT**, and flexibility to stretch to ~21:00–22:00 PKT a few days per week:

| Market | Their working day | Equals (PKT, winter) | Equals (PKT, summer) | Your daily overlap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **London** (GMT/BST) | 09:00–17:00 | 14:00–22:00 | 13:00–21:00 | **4–5 h** (13/14:00–18:00) |
| **Berlin/Amsterdam** (CET/CEST) | 09:00–17:00 | 13:00–21:00 | 12:00–20:00 | **5–6 h** |
| **New York** (EST/EDT) | 09:00–17:00 | 19:00–03:00 | 18:00–02:00 | **3–4 h evening** (19:00–22:00 PKT = their 09:00–12:00) |
| **San Francisco** (PST/PDT) | 09:00–17:00 | 22:00–06:00 | 21:00–05:00 | **1–2 h evening** — pitch async-first |
| **Dubai/Gulf** (GST, UTC+4) | 09:00–17:00 | 10:00–18:00 | 10:00–18:00 | **Full overlap** |

**DST reminder:** Europe/US shift clocks in March and November; Pakistan does not. Re-check overlap twice a year.

### Your magic window (memorize this)

> **08:00–17:00 UTC** = 13:00–22:00 PKT
> Covers: London 08:00–17:00 · Berlin 09:00–18:00 · New York 03:00–12:00 (i.e. their whole morning)

## 2. Where to state it (everywhere, consistently)

**CV header (already done):** `PKT (UTC+5) — 4+ hrs EU/US overlap`

**LinkedIn headline:** append `· Remote (UTC+5, EU/US overlap)`

**Email signature (use this exact format):**
```
Shahbaz Kareem — Principal Cloud & DevOps Architect
Lahore, Pakistan (PKT, UTC+5) | Core hours 08:00–17:00 UTC | Book a call: <cal.com or calendly link>
```

**Cover letters:** one sentence, always: *"Based in Lahore (UTC+5), I keep a 08:00–17:00 UTC core window — full overlap with EU working hours and US East mornings."*

## 3. Scripts for common situations

**Application form: "What are your working hours?"**
> Flexible. Core hours 08:00–17:00 UTC (covers EU business hours and US East mornings); I can extend evenings until 22:00 PKT for US-timezone teams when needed, and I'm used to async-first collaboration for anything outside overlap.

**Recruiter: "Are you able to work US hours?"**
> For US East I offer a daily 3–4 hour live overlap in my evening (your morning), plus full async coverage outside it. For US West I recommend async-first with a scheduled weekly sync — I've operated critical systems that way for years.

**They propose an interview at 23:00 PKT:** never complain. Reply:
> That time works for me. If it's ever easier on your side, I also have open slots 13:00–22:00 PKT (08:00–17:00 UTC) — here's my booking link.

**Proactive move in every interview:** when they ask "any questions for us?", include *"How does the team handle timezone spread — any core hours?"* It signals you have operated remotely before.

## 4. Prove it with tooling, not just words

| Tool | Action | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Cal.com / Calendly** | Free account, link in email signature + LinkedIn | Recruiters book instantly — zero back-and-forth |
| **Google Calendar** | Set working hours + allow "appointment slots" 08:00–17:00 UTC | Prevents accidental midnight bookings, shows discipline |
| **World Time Buddy** | Check before every interview | No "sorry, what time is that for you?" |
| **Slack/Discord** | Set timezone in profile on every platform | Small signal, noticed by remote-native teams |

## 5. The 3 habits that make timezone a non-issue

1. **Publish your overlap window before anyone asks** — in CV, LinkedIn, email signature, application forms. Silence makes hiring teams guess (and assume the worst).
2. **Never miss a written handoff.** End of your day = a status note for the EU/US person waking up: *"EOD PKT — deploys frozen, watching alert X, will have RCA draft by 09:00 PKT."* This is where the written-communication pack (`05-`) plugs in.
3. **Rotate the pain.** If the team is US-heavy, propose alternating late slots ("I'll take the late sync this sprint, can we alternate next sprint?"). Mature remote teams expect this; asking for it signals experience.

## 6. What NOT to do

- **Don't say "I can work any hours / night shift"** for an EU role — it reads as desperation and invites abuse. State the window; offer flexibility, not slavery.
- **Don't hide your location.** A "Remote" with no country reads as evasive; recruiters filter by timezone precisely because they want honesty.
- **Don't convert times verbally in interviews** — write them both ways in the chat: *"14:00 GMT / 19:00 PKT — confirmed on my side."* Written confirmation = communication skill on display.
